\section{Conclusion}
On a domain-disjoint EBBU/HP benchmark with a frozen, source-only learning protocol, lexical URL classifiers transferred well from EBBU to HP (F1 \res{F1ABLGBMALL} for LightGBM) but lost \res{DropBLGBMALL}--\res{DropBRFALL} F1 points from HP to EBBU (RQ1). Stability ranking gave higher target F1 than mean ranking at the same budget in the harder direction (\res{DeltaBARF} RF, \res{DeltaBALGBM} LightGBM), without a clear advantage in the other direction; reduced subsets did not match the full feature set across datasets (RQ2). Latency trade-offs depend on the model and execution setting (RQ3). Exploratory corrected SHAP comparisons pass strict numerical additivity while retaining descriptive, non-causal scope (RQ4). Exploratory baselines and error analysis on validation data (RQ5) show why: tree ensembles transfer far better than logistic regression, an HTTPS-only rule already explains much of the easy direction, and features whose class association reverses between collections cannot be identified by any source-only selector. DA-GAFS (RQ6) uses a second labelled collection to address this. Its directional gate found two robust reversals but did not improve transfer, because the strongest reversed cue remains useful to the tree ensembles; its cross-source importance ranking, however, gave a \res{hKI}-feature subset at \res{hRelI} of the extraction time that beat the source-only subset from HP to EBBU by \res{gDRISTB} (RF) and \res{gDLISTB} (LightGBM), and its ensemble rule avoided a harmful combination. The contribution is a reproducible empirical comparison under defined target-access constraints and an ablated, cost-aware selection framework, not a new classifier or a demonstrated improvement over UDA. Section~\ref{sec:future} outlines the next experiments.
